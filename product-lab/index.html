Offer lab

Take one case and turn it into an offer: the three levels, the attributes, the brand, the packaging, the services around it — and, because almost every offer today has a service inside, what is actually rented, who receives it and how it is delivered. Seven steps, one page out.

1The offer, and where it sits

Pick a case or write your own. Then say what need it satisfies — not what it is, what it does for somebody.

pure goodpure service
Why this mattersThe further right you sit, the less the buyer can judge before paying, and the more of your marketing has to be about evidence, people and process rather than about the thing itself.

2The three levels

Every offer is three offers at once. Write each one for your case.

Core benefit

What the buyer is really buying. A benefit, never a feature: not "a 50-minute class" but "the one hour of the day that is mine".

Actual product

What is delivered: features, design, quality level, brand name, packaging. The thing you can point at.

Augmented product

Everything around it: support, warranty, delivery, credit, after-sales, the app, the community.

Check the core benefitWhat you wrote reads like a feature or a thing. Ask "and what does that do for them?" one more time, and write that answer.

3Attributes: quality, features, design

Two different decisions hide inside "quality": the level you aim at, and the consistency with which you hit it. A cheap sandwich that is always the same has high consistency at a low level, and that is a strategy.

The testIf a feature has no "so that", it is a cost, not an attribute. Cut it or find its benefit.

4The brand

A brand is a name, a term, a sign, a symbol, a design or a combination of these that identifies the offer and separates it from the competition. Here you choose the elements, the positioning and the strategy.

Positioning: climb the ladder

The weakest positioning is on attributes, the strongest on beliefs and values. Write all three for your brand and keep the strongest as the one you would advertise.

5Packaging and label

If your offer has nothing physical, say so here and move on — but most services still have a physical carrier: a card, an app screen, a cup, a van.

Not a design questionMost of that list is law, not taste. The design decision is what you put next to it, and what you leave out.

7The service inside

A service is an economic activity offered by one party to another, where the value is intangible and the customer rents rather than owns: access to goods, labour, skills, facilities, networks or somebody's time. So the first question is never "what do we sell" but what are they renting.

The blueprint, cut down to size

Frontstage is what the customer sees; backstage is what has to happen for it to work. Then name the step most likely to fail, and the moment that decides whether they come back.

Frontstage: the customerBackstage: us
Zone of toleranceCustomers hold two levels in their head: the service they desire and the minimum they will accept. Between the two you are forgiven; below it you are not. Say in one line where your minimum sits.

This is what you hand in and what you present: one page, every decision, no slides needed. Fill in the names first.
