From the hypotheses of Pitch 1 to the document of Pitch 2, in the order the guide asks for. The page writes your instruments, reads your answers back, runs the analysis, carries the numbers into the mix, and gives you the whole thing as a Word document. Nothing leaves this browser.

How this works

You do not type your project in again. You hand the page what you already have, it writes the prompt that reads it, and from there each step feeds the next: the objectives come from Pitch 1, the discussion guide from the objectives, the questionnaire from what the interviews found, the analysis from your answers, and every mix decision from the analysis. Anything you prefer to write by hand, you write by hand, and the page starts from that instead.

Everything flows downwardsChange a box and what is built from it changes with it: the attributes and the competitors you set in step 3 are the questions of the survey and the rows of the map in step 4; the themes of step 2 are the agreement questions; the price levels decide the demand curve; and the Word file at the end is built from whatever the boxes say at the moment you press the button. If the questionnaire or the discussion guide is on screen, it rewrites itself as you type and tells you so underneath.
The two rulesNothing you paste leaves this browser, and nothing the assistant says is taken on trust: every number in the document comes from your own data, and every prompt on this page carries that data with it, so there is nothing for it to invent.

4.6 The costs, and 4.7 the price that makes most money

  
The model of the pricing session, after Phillips: the maximum price each person
  would pay gives a distribution; demand at a price is the maximum demand times the frequencies at
  or above it; profit is computed at every level and the best one wins.

Keeping your work - read this once
    
Closing the page, reloading it, or coming back the next day loses
      nothing: this page writes itself into your browser after every change and reads it back when
      it opens - it even survives a new version of the page being published. What it cannot survive
      is a different computer, so:

    
    
      
  1. 8.3 Download the draft file at the end of every session. That file is your project:
        it carries the boxes, the themes, the verdicts, the mix, and the survey answers, and it
        opens on any laptop with Load a draft file.
    2. 
      
  2. One person keeps it. Four people on four laptops means four different drafts: decide
        who holds the file, and pass it around after each session instead of typing things twice.
    3. 
      
  3. To work in parallel, divide by section and then merge. One takes the product, another
        the price, another place and promotion. Each downloads their own draft file; one person loads
        them one after the other with Load or merge a draft file. Merging never wipes what is on
        your page: empty boxes are filled from the file, and anything you both wrote differently is
        listed for you to choose. Two people in the same browser (two tabs on one laptop) do
        overwrite each other - the page warns you when it sees a second tab.
    4. 
      
  4. The page also saves itself into this browser every few seconds, which is enough
        between one day and the next on the same machine - and only there.
    5. 
      
  5. What loses the work: a private or incognito window, clearing the site data or
        history, another laptop, another browser, and a shared computer somebody else cleans. None of
        it reaches a server, so there is nothing to recover: the downloaded file is the backup.
    6.
