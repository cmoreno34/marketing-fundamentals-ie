The twelve archetypes

Jung found twelve characters that recur in every culture. Brands that are easy to recognise lean on one of them. Pick a brand, answer five questions about how it behaves, choose its character, and write the traits, the values and the one line that go with it.

1The brand

A brand everybody in the room knows works best for learning; your own case brand works best for your project. Pick one or type it.

Two examples from the slideMercedes — leadership, success, importance — sits in the Ruler. Dove — innocence and optimism — sits in the Innocent. Neither of them chose twelve characters: they chose one.

2The diagnostic: what moves this brand?

Five questions, one click each. Answer for the brand as it behaves, not as it would like to be seen. The four columns are the four motivations of the model: Freedom, Ego, Social and Order - and there is no right answer, only the one that fits this brand.

3The archetype

No, a brand is not locked into one characterReal brands usually lean on one and borrow from another: Mercedes is a Ruler with a bit of Creator in the engineering; Nike is a Hero with a bit of Outlaw. What you choose here is the primary: the one it is recognised by, the one that decides the tone when the two disagree. The secondary is optional and goes below. The only thing that does not work is three or four at once — then nobody can tell what the brand is like.

Answer the five questions and the three archetypes of the leading motivation appear here, with what each one promises and what it fears. Pick the primary one.

Leave it at none if the brand is pure. If you pick one, the primary still wins whenever the two would say different things.

4Traits and voice

Pick five adjectives, no more. The ones that fit your archetype are outlined; the rest are still allowed, but a trait that contradicts the character is a decision, not an accident.

5Brand values

Pick three to five from the list, then write the behaviour that proves each one. A value with no behaviour behind it is a poster, and the room can tell.

The test of a valueIf the opposite is unthinkable for any brand in the category — nobody claims to be dishonest — the value says nothing. Keep the ones a competitor could honestly refuse to sign.

6The essence line

One line that says what the brand is about, in three to six words. Like the ones in the chapter: Coca-Cola — sharing and happiness, Apple — empowering people through technology, BMW — sheer driving pleasure.

The test of the lineCover the logo and read it to somebody. If it could be any brand in the category, it is a slogan, not an essence.
