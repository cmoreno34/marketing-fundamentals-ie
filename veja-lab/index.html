The survey

A group of students designed and ran this survey for VAJA, a carbonated drink built around birch sap: allergy relief, immune boost and relaxation without caffeine. They asked 18 rating questions on a 1 to 5 scale and 7 demographic questions, and got 77 complete answers.

That is a small sample and a self-selected one: everything you find here is a hypothesis about the market, not a measurement of it. It is still enough to practise every analysis of the session, and to see what a real dataset looks like when it arrives.

Describe: one variable at a time

Analysingthe VAJA case

For a 1 to 5 rating: the mean, how spread the answers are, and the share who answered 4 or 5 — the top-two box, which is what a client reads. For a category: the frequency table.

What these columns are. n: how many people answered that question. mean: the average answer, from 1 to 5. sd: how spread the answers are around the average — under 0.8 the room agrees, above 1.2 it is split. top 2: the percentage who answered 4 or 5, which is the number a client reads. bottom 2: the percentage who answered 1 or 2, which is the objection you will have to deal with.

Categories

Cross: does the answer depend on the group?

Analysingthe VAJA case

Two categorical variables. Row percentages, expected counts, chi-square, and the validity check before the p-value. To cross a rating, the lab bands it into low (1-2), middle (3) and high (4-5).

Or look at one pair

What a crosstab is. One question split by another: the answers of each group, side by side. The percentage is read inside each row — of the people in that group. Chi-square asks whether a gap that size would appear by chance; p is how often it would. Cramer's V says how strong the link is, from 0 to 1. And before any of that, the expected counts: with fewer than 5 expected in most cells the test cannot be read at all.

Segment: k-means on the ratings

Analysingthe VAJA case

The 18 rating questions describe what each respondent wants. K-means groups the people whose answers look alike, and the centroid table tells you what each group is. Choose how many segments and read the table, not the algorithm.

What segmentation does. It groups the people whose answers look alike. The centroid table is the average answer of each group to every question: the rows where the groups are furthest apart are what separates them, and what you name them after. The silhouette scores how well separated the groups are, from -1 to 1: above 0.5 is a clear structure, 0.25 to 0.5 is weak but usable, below 0.25 means the groups are mostly an artefact of asking for them. Variance explained is how much of the differences between people the grouping accounts for.
